Step-by-Step: Inheriting Property A Through the Trust (~2040–2050)

Assumes Property A is held in the Owner’s revocable living trust and the Heir is a trust beneficiary. Trust administration generally happens without court probate; probate is only needed for assets outside the trust. Deadlines below are from official California sources.

Phase 1 — Immediately at death (Days 0–60)

  1. Obtain certified death certificates (order several from the county vital records / CA Dept. of Public Health).
  2. Successor trustee accepts the role — sign an Acceptance of Trusteeship and/or Certification of Trust (Probate Code §18100.5) to prove authority to banks, title companies, and agencies. [CA Courts — Probate Trusts]
  3. Serve the Probate Code §16061.7 notice to all beneficiaries and heirs within 60 days of death; this starts the 120-day window for trust contests. [CA Courts Self-Help] [Prob. Code §16061.7 (leginfo)]
  4. Secure and inventory all trust assets and record date-of-death values — this is critical for the IRC §1014 step-up in basis. [IRS Pub. 551]
  5. Notify the county assessor of the real-property transfer within 150 days of death (change-in-ownership notice). [CA Courts — Probate Trusts]

Phase 2 — Title & property tax (Days 0–365)

  1. Record the Affidavit of Death of Trustee (with certified death certificate) and the successor trustee’s Consent in the county where Property A is located; record the Preliminary Change of Ownership Report (PCOR) at the same time. [Santa Clara County Assessor] [BOE change-in-ownership FAQ]
  2. Decide the use of Property A within 1 year — this is the Prop 19 fork:
    • Move in as primary residence within 1 year → file homeowners’ exemption BOE-266 within 1 year and claim BOE-19-P within 3 years → assessed value = parent’s factored base year value + any excess above (FBYV + $1,044,586 cap, 2025–27 amount). [BOE Prop 19] [BOE Fact Sheet]
    • Rent it out or leave vacant → no exclusion; full reassessment to date-of-death market value. [BOE Prop 19 FAQ]
    • Sell it → reassessment occurs, but the IRC §1014 step-up makes the sale gain ≈ $0; a supplemental assessment bill may arrive after the sale (escrow handles it). [IRS Pub. 551]
  3. Get a date-of-death appraisal of Property A from a certified appraiser to document the stepped-up basis. [IRS Pub. 551]

Phase 3 — Taxes (Year of death and following)

  1. Federal estate tax (Form 706): required only if gross estate + adjusted taxable gifts exceed the exemption — $15,000,000 per person for 2026 deaths, permanent and indexed from 2027 (P.L. 119-21). If below, no Form 706 is needed. If married and portability of the spouse’s unused exemption is wanted, a timely Form 706 election is still required. [IRS estate tax FAQ] [Rev. Proc. 2025-32]
  2. California: no state estate or inheritance tax return and no tax due — inheritance is not income. [CA FTB]
  3. Final personal income tax return (Form 1040) for the Owner, plus a fiduciary return (Form 1041) for the trust after it becomes irrevocable; obtain a new EIN for the trust. [CA Courts — Probate Trusts] [IRS Form 1041]
  4. Inherited retirement accounts: no step-up; non-spouse beneficiaries generally must distribute within 10 years (SECURE Act 2.0) and pay ordinary income tax — a key reason the cash portion may be taxed while the house is not. [IRS retirement plans]

Phase 4 — Distribution & the live / rent / sell decision (Months 6–24)

  1. Pay debts, taxes, and administration expenses, keep full accountings, and distribute per the trust terms. [CA Courts Self-Help]
  2. Record the new deed transferring Property A from trustee to Heir (documentary transfer tax may apply unless an exemption is claimed; parent–child transfers have an exemption pathway). [SF/County recorder — transfer tax] [BOE]
  3. Update insurance, utilities, and property-tax billing to the Heir’s name; re-price homeowners (≈$1,570/yr CA avg) vs. landlord (≈$1,700/yr CA avg) coverage depending on use. [CA DOI]
  4. Execute the decision using the Decision Analysis: sell & reinvest (recommended), live in (Prop 19 partial exclusion if moved in within 1 year), or rent (full reassessment).
