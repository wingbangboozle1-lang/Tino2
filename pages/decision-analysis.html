Reasoning & recommendation

  1. Sell & reinvest wins on the numbers. The §1014 step-up makes the sale essentially tax-free, and ≈$6,132,180 reinvested at a conservative 7% yields ≈$300,480/yr after tax — roughly 12–25× the after-tax income from renting Property A, because renting triggers a full Prop 19 reassessment (tax ≈$86,870/yr in 2045), which consumes ≈60% of gross rent before any other expense.
  2. Living in Property A is a lifestyle choice, not a profit-maximizing one. It costs ≈$91,520/yr (2045) after the partial Prop 19 exclusion. Moving in within 1 year still saves ≈$13,890/yr in property tax vs. renting (assessed $5,486,650 vs $6,531,230), so if the Heir wants to live there, do it — but it forgoes both the rental income and the investment yield.
  3. Property B stays. It is already owned (no reassessment event), produces ≈$71,680/yr NOI, and the owner-occupied unit means no separate rent is needed — keep it in every scenario.
  4. Estate tax is likely a non-issue: unless the total estate (both properties + the “considerable” cash/investments) exceeds $15,000,000 per person in 2026 dollars (indexed from 2027), no federal estate tax is due; CA adds nothing. If the estate does exceed it, the excess is taxed at 40% federal — plan gifts ($19,000/yr/recipient) or trust structures with counsel. [Rev. Proc. 2025-32] [FTB]
  5. Timing lever: if the Heir wants the Prop 19 benefit and to live there, the 1-year move-in and BOE-266/BOE-19-P filings are hard deadlines measured from the date of death — build them into the trust-administration checklist. [BOE]

Bottom line

Recommended plan: At inheritance (~2040–2050), sell Property A (step-up basis ⇒ ≈$0 capital gains tax), pay ≈$399,050 selling costs, and reinvest ≈$6,132,180 (2045 base case) for ≈$300,480/yr after-tax income at a conservative 7% return. Keep Property B as the owner-occupied rental (≈$71,680/yr NOI). If living in Cupertino is desired, move in within 1 year of death and file BOE-266 + BOE-19-P to keep the partial exclusion (≈$13,890/yr tax savings vs. renting).
