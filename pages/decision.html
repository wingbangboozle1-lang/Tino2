Recommendation

Sell the Cupertino house shortly after inheritance (modeled year 2045). Reinvest the net proceeds (≈ $5,662,110 after a 5% selling-cost assumption). Keep the San Francisco home as primary residence plus rental (2 of 3 units).

Why this maximizes profit and lowers cost:

  • Property tax is the dominant cost. Even with Proposition 19 relief (moving in within 1 year and filing BOE-19-P), the Cupertino home reassesses to ≈ $4,915,530 at 2045 values → ≈ $61,440/yr property tax. Without Prop 19 relief (renting it out), full reassessment → ≈ $74,500/yr. Total cost of living there ≈ $110,450/yr once insurance, water, and maintenance are added.
  • Renting is a poor return. Projected net ≈ $26,120/yr before income tax on a $5,960,120 asset (≈ 0.4% yield) — because full reassessment applies to a non-owner-occupied rental under Prop 19.
  • Selling is nearly tax-free. Step-up in basis (IRC § 1014) sets the cost basis at date-of-death fair market value, so selling promptly produces ≈ $0 taxable gain. The § 121 exclusion ($250,000/$500,000) is an additional option if the heirs move in for 2 of 5 years before a later sale.
  • Invested proceeds outperform holding. ≈ $5,662,110 invested at a 6% planning assumption yields ≈ $339,730 in year one and ≈ $608,400/yr by 2055 — several times the net rental cash flow — with no property tax, insurance, or maintenance burden.
  • Keep the SF property. It already combines primary-residence use with ≈ $150,480/yr gross rent (2 units) against a low assessed-value tax bill. Projected net at 2045 ≈ $102,500/yr before income tax.
  • Estate tax. No federal estate tax if the total estate is under $15,000,000 per person ($30,000,000 per couple with portability, 2026 IRS figures); California has no estate or inheritance tax. Projected combined house values at 2045 ≈ $9,116,420 — below the exclusion unless the inherited money ([DOLLAR AMOUNT]) pushes the estate over the threshold. Above it, the estate pays 40% on the excess before distribution (paid by the estate, not heirs).
