Step-Up in Basis, Capital Gains & Rental Tax

All rules below are confirmed IRS publications or Internal Revenue Code sections.

1. Step-up in basis — inherited property

RuleDetailsSource
Inherited basisFair market value at the decedent's date of death (alternate valuation date 6 months after death if the executor elects and it lowers both gross estate and estate tax)IRS Publication 551 (Basis of Assets); IRC § 1014(a)
Pre-death appreciationNot taxed to the heir; gain is measured only from the date-of-death value at a later saleIRC § 1014; IRS Pub 551
Holding periodInherited property is treated as long-term regardless of how long the heir holds itIRC § 1223(9); IRS Publication 559
California community propertyAt the first spouse's death, the entire community property (including the surviving spouse's half) generally steps up to date-of-death value ("double step-up"); California is a community property stateIRS Publication 551
DocumentationDate-of-death appraisal recommended for real estate to support basisIRS guidance (secondary summaries)

2. Selling an inherited home — capital gains

RuleDetailsSource
Taxable gain on saleSale price − stepped-up basis − costs of sale; long-term capital gains rates (0%/15%/20% by income)IRS
Sale soon after deathTaxable gain ≈ $0 if sold at or near date-of-death value (basis stepped up)IRC § 1014; IRS Pub 551
Primary-residence exclusion (IRC § 121)Up to $250,000 gain excluded (single) or $500,000 (married filing jointly) if owned AND used as main home ≥ 2 of the 5 years before sale; limits not inflation-adjusted; once every 2 yearsIRS Publication 523; IRS Topic No. 701
Rental use before saleGain allocated to nonqualified (rental) use after 2008 may be taxable; depreciation recaptured up to 25%IRS Publications 523/527

3. Keeping an inherited home as a rental

RuleDetailsSource
Rental incomeReported on Schedule E; taxed at ordinary income ratesIRS Publication 527
DepreciationResidential rental building: straight-line over 27.5 years (MACRS GDS); land is not depreciable; only the rental portion of a mixed-use property is depreciableIRS Publication 527
Repairs vs. improvementsRepairs deductible when paid; improvements capitalized and depreciatedIRS Publication 527
Depreciation recapture at saleRecaptured up to 25% (unrecaptured Section 1250 gain)IRS Publications 527/544
Passive activity lossesRental losses generally passive — deductible against passive income; unused losses carry forward (real-estate-professional exception)IRS Publication 527
Property tax deductionDeductible as rental expense (Schedule E); personal portion subject to $10,000 SALT cap on itemized returnsIRS

4. Why this favors selling the Cupertino house after inheritance

  • Step-up in basis erases the parents' decades of appreciation — a sale soon after the transfer produces ≈ $0 taxable gain (rounded to nearest $10).
  • If instead the heirs keep and later sell, tax is due on post-inheritance appreciation (and recaptured depreciation if rented).
  • If the heirs move in and live there 2 of 5 years before selling, the § 121 exclusion ($250,000/$500,000) applies on top of the stepped-up basis.
