Internal Revenue Service
Gifts and inheritances (FAQ)		The basis of inherited property is generally its fair market value on the date of death (or on the alternate valuation date if the executor elects it). “If you sell the property for more than your basis, you have a taxable gain.”VerifiedIRS
Internal Revenue Service
Topic no. 409, Capital gains and losses		Long-term gains are taxed at 0%, 15% or 20%. Gain from depreciation on real property (unrecaptured section 1250 gain) is taxed at a maximum of 25%. Inherited property is treated as held long term. Losses on personal-use property are not deductible.VerifiedIRS
Internal Revenue Service
Topic no. 701, Sale of your home		Up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 on a joint return) on a main home is excluded if it was owned and lived in for at least two of the five years before the sale.VerifiedIRS
Internal Revenue Service
Topic no. 559, Net investment income tax		3.8% on the lesser of net investment income or income above $250,000 (joint) / $200,000 (single). Rents and gains from real estate count; gain excluded on a home sale does not.VerifiedIRS
Internal Revenue Service
Topic no. 703, Basis of assets		Basis is used to figure depreciation and gain or loss, and is reduced by depreciation allowed.VerifiedIRS

Proposition 19

Proposition 19
PageWhat was read thereStatusSource
California State Board of Equalization
Proposition 19 — fact sheets, questions and answers, forms checklist		Parent-to-child exclusion applies only to the parent’s principal residence that becomes the child’s principal residence. Worked example: taxable value $300,000, market value $1,500,000 → new taxable value $500,000. A rented home does not qualify. Homeowners’ exemption claim “Within 1 year from date of death or transfer”; exclusion claim “Within 3 years from date of death or transfer, or before transfer to 3rd party, whichever is earlier.” If the child stops living there, the home is taxed on its market value at the date of inheritance, adjusted for inflation.VerifiedBoard of Equalization
California State Board of Equalization
Letter to Assessors 2025/009		The $1 million amount is $1,044,586 for transfers from 16 February 2025 to 15 February 2027 (previously $1,022,600; before that $1,000,000). It is adjusted every other 16 February by the change in the FHFA California house price index for the prior calendar year (2.15% for 2024).VerifiedBoard of Equalization
California State Board of Equalization
Letter to Assessors 2026/026 — intergenerational transfer exclusion questions and answers		The home must become the child’s principal residence within one year: “There are no exceptions to this rule.” A trust “must be looked through to determine who has present beneficial ownership”. Property moved into a legal entity such as an LLC is not eligible. Of two homes kept by the same child, “only one can qualify as the child’s principal residence”. In a multi-unit property “only the unit occupied as the principal residence qualifies”.VerifiedBoard of Equalization
Santa Clara County Assessor
Parent to child transfers (Proposition 19)		“The value limit is equal to the home’s taxable value at time of transfer plus $1 million. Any amount of market value exceeding the limit is added to the taxable value for the transferee.” Example: $200,000 taxable value, $1,800,000 market value → $800,000.VerifiedSanta Clara Assessor
