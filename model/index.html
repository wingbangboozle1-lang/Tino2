Summary

Two homes and one inheritance expected between 2040 and 2050. The answer comes first. Every figure below links to the official page it came from, or to the list of things that still need checking.

Short answer

Property A · Cupertino · to be inherited

Sell it soon after inheriting and invest the money — unless a family member wants to live in it.

  • At death the tax basis resets to the house’s value that day, so a prompt sale leaves $0 of taxable gain.
  • Kept as a rental, the house is re-taxed on its full market value: about $81,760 a year in 2045, which leaves about $22,300 of rent after costs on a house worth $6,886,080.
  • Sold, the same money earns about $519,780 in its first year at the middle return (8.03%), before tax.
  • Living in it is a choice with a price: about $218,590 a year over 10 years compared with selling ($127,920 in 2026 dollars).

Property B · San Francisco · the household’s home

Keep it.

  • The two rented units leave about $67,880 a year after costs, and the household lives in the third.
  • Its property tax is $2,440 a year. A buyer would pay about $22,360 on the same building. That advantage ends for good when it is sold.
  • To do as well after a sale, the money left would have to earn 9.5% to 12.9% a year. The middle return in this model is 8.03%.

These are money results. They do not put a price on what the family wants from either home. One open question could change the picture for Property B: who holds its title today.

What would change the answer

Low investment returns

Selling stops being best if the invested money earns less than 4.7% a year (against renting) or 4.1% (against living there). Treasury securities have paid 4.65% a year since 1987.

Fast house-price growth

Across three house-price histories and three return histories, selling is best in 7 of 9 combinations. The others: middle house growth with treasuries returns (rent it out wins); high house growth with treasuries returns (rent it out wins).

Someone wants to live there

Moving in within one year keeps part of the old tax base. In 2045 that saves about $21,420 a year in property tax against renting it out, for as long as the heir lives there.

Do these first

  1. Check two documents now. The deed to Property B (who owns it) and the trust that holds Property A (what it allows).
  2. Protect the exclusion. Keep Property A as [OWNER]’s principal residence. Do not rent it out and do not move it into an LLC.
  3. Add up the whole estate. Property A alone is far below the federal exclusion; the “considerable amount of money” is what decides whether estate tax applies. See the room left under the exclusion.
  4. At the death, get an appraisal dated that day and follow the deadlines: 60 days, 150 days, nine months, one year, three years.
  5. Decide within the first year whether anyone will live in Property A. The one-year rule has no exceptions.
