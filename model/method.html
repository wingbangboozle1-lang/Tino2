Method

How the figures are produced, every input with its value and origin, the simplifications, and how to rebuild and re-check the pages.

How the model works

  1. Values. Each home’s market value grows at a fixed yearly rate from the owner’s estimate for November 2026.
  2. Property tax. Bill = tax rate × taxed value + fixed charges. Taxed value rises 2% a year until ownership changes. Fixed charges are the 2025 bill minus rate × assessed value, and rise with inflation.
  3. Inheritance. The death is placed on 1 January of the chosen year. With no exclusion the taxed value becomes the market value. With the parent-to-child exclusion it becomes market value minus the Proposition 19 amount (never less than the old taxed value).
  4. Proposition 19 amount. Starts at the official figure and is raised on 16 February of each odd-numbered year by one year of the scenario’s California house-price change.
  5. Sell and invest. Sale at the value at death, less selling cost; no gain. The proceeds grow at the investment return. At the end the growth is taxed once at the gains rate.
  6. Rent it out. Rent grows with inflation, less vacancy. Costs: property tax on full value, insurance, repairs, manager. Depreciation is the building share of the value at death over 27.5 years; a loss is carried forward and used against later rent or the final gain. Yearly cash goes into the same kind of investment account. At the end the house is sold: depreciation is taxed back at the recapture rate, the rest of the gain at the gains rate.
  7. Live in it. Costs: property tax with the exclusion, insurance, repairs, water and power. Income: rent, after tax, from the unit left in Property B, starting at the market level and then rising at 60% of inflation. The yearly shortfall is drawn from an investment account, so its cost includes the growth given up. At the end the house is sold; the first $500,000 of gain is not taxed.
  8. Comparison. All three are measured at the same date after selling everything and paying tax. “Yearly price of living in it” is the level yearly amount that, invested at the same return, would close the gap to selling.
  9. Estate tax. 40% of the estate above the exclusion. The exclusion is official for 2026 and 2027 and then grows at 2.53% a year. It is the same under all three choices and is shown separately.
  10. Rounding. Calculations keep full precision; every dollar figure shown in the analysis is rounded to the nearest $10. Official amounts on the reference pages are quoted exactly.

Simplifications

  • One flat tax rate for each kind of income; no brackets, deductions, or alternative minimum tax. Investment growth is taxed once, at the end.
  • The loss made by selling the inherited house (the selling cost) is not counted as a tax saving. That makes “sell and invest” look slightly worse than it is.
  • No mortgage on either property, no earthquake insurance, no large one-off repairs, no cost of moving.
  • Property B’s own rent, costs and value are the same under every choice for Property A and are left out of that comparison. Losing Property B’s $7,000 homeowners’ exemption when the household moves is ignored (under $100 a year).
  • A rental loss is used against the final gain at the gain’s rate rather than against other income.
  • Rent on the unit left in Property B rises at the allowed rate for the whole period, as if the first tenant stayed.
  • The federal estate tax is computed without deductions or lifetime gifts.

No packages are installed and nothing is loaded from other websites. The calculation code is model/src/engine.js; the same file runs in the browser calculator. Built 2026-11-10 07:03 UTC.

No personal information. People appear only as [OWNER], [HEIR] and [TRUSTEE]; properties only as A and B. The pages contain no names, addresses, parcel numbers or contact details.
