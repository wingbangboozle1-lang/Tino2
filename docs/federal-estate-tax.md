# Federal Estate / Inheritance Tax — Research Record

## Summary (verified against IRS sources)
- United States has a federal ESTATE tax (paid by the estate), NOT a federal inheritance tax (paid by the heir). There is no federal inheritance tax.
- For deaths in calendar year 2026: basic exclusion amount = $15,000,000 per person. Top estate tax rate = 40% (applies only to value above the exclusion, after deductions and prior taxable gifts).
- Applicable credit amount for 2026: $5,945,800.
- Portability: a surviving spouse can use a deceased spouse's unused exclusion (DSUE) by filing Form 706 and electing portability → a married couple can shield up to $30,000,000 combined in 2026.
- The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), Public Law 119-21, signed July 4, 2025 (Section 70106), set the exclusion at $15,000,000 for 2026, removed the scheduled sunset, and made the higher level permanent. Inflation indexing resumes for deaths in 2027 and later (base year 2025).
- Form 706 (federal estate tax return) is due 9 months after date of death; automatic 6-month extension via Form 4768. Required only when the estate exceeds the filing threshold.
- Inherited cash/property is generally NOT taxable income to the heir (no federal inheritance tax). Income earned AFTER inheritance (rent, dividends, sale gains above stepped-up basis) is taxable.
