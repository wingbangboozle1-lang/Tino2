# Research: Step-Up in Basis, Capital Gains, and Rental Income Tax

**Prepared by:** session-arena | **Sources verified:** yes (IRS publications and code sections)

## 1. Step-up in basis — inherited property

| Item | Rule | Source | Verified |
|------|------|--------|----------|
| Basis of inherited property | Generally fair market value at the decedent's date of death (alternate valuation date 6 months after death if executor elects and it lowers both gross estate and estate tax) | IRC § 1014(a); IRS Publication 551 (Basis of Assets) | Y |
| Appreciation during decedent's life | Not taxed to the heir; gain measured only from date-of-death value at later sale | IRC § 1014; IRS Publication 551 | Y |
| Holding period | Inherited property is treated as long-term regardless of how long the heir holds it | IRC § 1223(9); IRS Publication 559 | Y |
| California community property | At first spouse's death, the entire community property (including surviving spouse's half) generally receives a step-up to date-of-death value ("double step-up"); California is a community property state | IRS Publication 551 | Y |
| Documentation | Date-of-death appraisal recommended for real estate to support basis | IRS guidance (secondary summaries) | Y |

## 2. Selling an inherited home — capital gains

| Item | Rule | Source | Verified |
|------|------|--------|----------|
| Taxable gain on sale | Sale price − stepped-up basis (date-of-death FMV) − costs of sale; long-term capital gains rates (0%/15%/20% by income) apply | IRS | Y |
| Primary-residence exclusion (IRC § 121) | Up to $250,000 gain excluded (single) or $500,000 (married filing jointly) if owned AND used as main home ≥ 2 of the 5 years before sale; limits not inflation-adjusted; once every 2 years | IRS Publication 523; IRS Topic No. 701 | Y |
| Inherited home sold soon after death | Gain ≈ $0 if sold at or near date-of-death value (step-up resets basis) | IRS § 1014 | Y |
| Rental use before sale | Portions of gain allocated to nonqualified (rental) use after 2008 may be taxable; depreciation recapture up to 25% | IRS Publications 523/527 | Y |

## 3. Keeping an inherited home as a rental

| Item | Rule | Source | Verified |
|------|------|--------|----------|
| Rental income | Reported on Schedule E, taxed at ordinary income rates | IRS Publication 527 | Y |
| Depreciation | Residential rental building: straight-line over 27.5 years (MACRS GDS); land not depreciable; only the rental portion of a mixed-use property is depreciable | IRS Publication 527 | Y |
| Repairs vs. improvements | Repairs deductible when paid; improvements capitalized and depreciated | IRS Publication 527 | Y |
| Depreciation recapture at sale | Recaptured up to 25% (unrecaptured Section 1250 gain) | IRS Publications 527/544 | Y |
| Passive activity losses | Rental losses generally passive; deductible against passive income; unused losses carry forward (real-estate-professional exception exists) | IRS Publication 527 | Y |
| Property tax on rental | Deductible as rental expense (Schedule E) subject to SALT cap ($10,000) on itemized returns for the personal portion | IRS | Y |
