| Item | Value / rule | Source | Verified |
|------|-------------|--------|----------|
| Basic exclusion amount (deaths in 2026) | $15,000,000 per person | IRS, "Working Families Tax Cuts" (irs.gov newsroom); Rev. Proc. 2025-32; P.L. 119-21 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 4, 2025) | Y |

**Note on permanence:** Some secondary sources describe the $15M level as "permanent"; one source notes a possible December 31, 2034 sunset under different readings of the law. Planning should assume current law and re-verify nearer the inheritance date.

## 3. What this means for the estate

- Federal estate tax is owed ONLY by the estate (not heirs), and only on the portion above $15M per person ($30M per couple with portability), at 40%.
- If the combined estate (both houses + money) is below $15M per person: no federal estate tax, no California estate/inheritance tax.
- If above the threshold: estate pays 40% on the excess before distributions. Heirs receive assets income-tax-free at the state level; the inheritance itself is not income to them.
- The "considerable amount of money" is unspecified — placeholders used. If it pushes the estate near/above $15M per person, estate planning (portability Form 706, gifting within annual exclusions, trusts) should be reviewed with an estate attorney before the transfer.

## 4. Pending / discussed law changes
- No enacted federal or California change to estate/inheritance tax thresholds as of Nov 2026 beyond P.L. 119-21 (already reflected in the $15M figure).
- Proposals such as the "For the 99.5 Percent Act" (lowered exemption, graduated rates) did not enact. Monitor annually; exemption level at the actual year of death controls.
