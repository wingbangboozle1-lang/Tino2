## Key tax findings (VERIFIED against official sources)
- Federal estate tax exclusion 2026: $15,000,000 per person ($30,000,000 married couple with portability); top rate 40% above; permanent under OBBBA (Public Law 119-21), indexed for inflation after 2026. (IRS Form 706 instructions / Rev. Proc. 2025-32)
- California: NO estate tax, NO inheritance tax; inheritance is not taxable income to heirs. (CA Franchise Tax Board)
- Capital gains: inherited property gets a stepped-up basis to date-of-death value (IRC §1014); selling soon after inheritance → little/no capital gains tax. CA taxes gains as ordinary income (up to 13.3%). (IRS Pub. 551/559; CA FTB)
- Proposition 13: property tax = 1% of assessed value + voter-approved bonds; assessed value rises max 2%/year without ownership change. (CA State Board of Equalization)
- Proposition 19: to keep a low assessment on an inherited home, the child must live in it as primary residence within 1 year and file BOE-19-P; cap is FBYV + $1,044,586 (current indexed amount); rentals are fully reassessed. (CA Board of Equalization)

## Working conclusion (revisit at inheritance with verified figures)
1. Keep the San Francisco house as a rental — very low Prop 13 tax and strong positive modeled cash flow; owner can live there as primary residence and still collect rent.
2. Sell the Cupertino house at inheritance unless an heir needs a primary residence there — step-up basis minimizes capital gains; keeping it triggers a large Prop 19 reassessment ($57,090–$70,670/yr modeled tax).
3. If an heir will live in the Cupertino home, move in within 1 year and file BOE-19-P + homeowner's exemption to soften the tax increase.
4. Federal estate tax likely $0 if the total estate (houses + [INHERITANCE_ASSETS]) stays under $15,000,000 per person ($30,000,000 couple); amounts above are taxed at 40%.
