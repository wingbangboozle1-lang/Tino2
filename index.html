Executive summary

The answer depends on one variable

Selling wins the base case. It is not robust to a bad 30 years.

On the central assumptions a sale of Property A at inheritance, with the proceeds invested, ends 20 years later at $11.10M. Renting ends at $7.73M and living in it at $6.16M. That ranking holds for home-price growth from −2% to about 6.3% a year, and for portfolio returns from 3% to 9%.

But resampling the actual 1976-2025 record of California inflation and Santa Clara County house prices gives renting the higher real outcome in 43.9% of 2,000 thirty-year histories. Selling is tightly clustered ($7.82M to $8.18M at the 10th to 90th percentile); renting spans $3.42M to $15.50M. Selling buys certainty, not the highest expected value.

Recommendation: treat selling as the risk-controlled default and renting as the higher-variance alternative. The decision should wait on the six critical facts listed on the questions page; three of them can reverse the ranking on their own.
