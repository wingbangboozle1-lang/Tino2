## Findings for review — no implementation made

1. **High — historical public exposure remains unresolved.** The repository is public, and the repository's own incident notes say that older Git history, branches, pull requests, caches, forks or clones may retain pre-rounding material. Current-tree deletion, Pages exclusions, `robots.txt`, and the passing scan do not purge those copies. This session did not rewrite history or change visibility: a branch-local rewrite cannot clean every remote ref, fork, cache, or clone, and the platform-provided branch is not an administrator-controlled cleanup. The GitHub procedure for sensitive-data incidents is [Removing sensitive data from a repository](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/keeping-your-account-and-data-secure/removing-sensitive-data-from-a-repository). The site's Flagged page already carries the incident and owner/admin runbook. Treat cleanup as **not done** until an authorized administrator coordinates it and verifies the remote refs and exposure response.
2. **High — the public site publishes rounded household-derived amounts.** The live executive summary says its property and rent figures are owner-stated; the Properties page explains the figures are rounded and unverified. Rounding may reduce precision but cannot guarantee non-identifiability. The current privacy scan only checks known tokens and common patterns. Because the brief says privacy is the highest priority, an owner decision is still needed on whether rounded household-derived values may remain public at all. Official rules do not validate these household inputs. Keep any appraisal, tax bill, lease, insurance quote, estate inventory, account record or exact private input off the public site and out of this repository.
3. **Medium — mixed fictional / household framing needs clearer separation.** The live header labels the guide “FICTIONAL EXAMPLES”; the homepage/executive summary also display household-stated figures and a household-derived conditional conclusion, while the Compare page describes its scenario inputs as invented. Readers may not know which figures drive the recommendation versus which are teaching examples. Proposed fix for review: label household-stated history, invented calculator assumptions and model outputs as three distinct data classes on every relevant page; name the exact input set used by each recommendation; ensure the headline does not attribute a synthetic comparison's result to household facts. This is a presentation/suggestion item, not silently implemented here.
4. **Decision readiness: not ready for a final choice.** The public Questions register appropriately leaves private answers unfilled. Before relying on a path, obtain and review the trust/title documents, date-of-death appraisal and basis work, Prop 19 eligibility/claim timing, complete estate and prior-gift/portability inventory, Property A tax-rate area and actual bill, leases/local-use constraints for Property B, independent rents/appraisals, coverage quotes, debt and five-year capital budgets, and a household occupancy/investment policy. The public register identifies custodians and discovery steps. These are not facts that an official webpage can resolve for the household.
5. **Date-sensitive law and market inputs are snapshots, not 2040–2050 forecasts.** The site links the controlling source pages for manual review; re-open them near the transfer and before a sale. Relevant official starting points: [IRS estate tax](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-tax), [Instructions for Form 706](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i706), [IRS inherited basis FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/interest-dividends-other-types-of-income/gifts-inheritances/gifts-inheritances), [California BOE Proposition 19](https://www.boe.ca.gov/prop19/), [California FTB forms and publications](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/), and [Santa Clara County property tax](https://www.santaclaracountyca.org/Property_Tax.html).
