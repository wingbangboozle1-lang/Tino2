Recommendation

Sell Property A within about a year of inheriting it, reinvest the proceeds in a diversified mix, and keep Property B as the family home and income property.

  1. The tax reset makes the day of inheritance the cheapest day to sell. The Heir's basis becomes the market value at death, so a prompt sale leaves little or no capital gain to tax.
  2. The low property tax does not survive. In 2045 (base scenario) the bill goes from about $10,800 to $81,530 a year if rented, or $62,970 if the Heir moves in.
  3. As a rental, Property A earns very little on its value. Benchmark rent of $118,120 less tax, insurance and upkeep leaves about $25,040 a year, 0.36% of a $6,888,060 asset. Nearly all its return would have to come from price growth.
  4. Selling wins unless the money is parked in savings. With the mixed or stock-index return, selling is ahead in 18 of 18 cases. At the 4.65% government-securities return it is ahead in only 3 of 9, the low-growth ones.
  5. Property B already covers housing and income. It houses the family in the third unit and collects $111,320 a year by 2045; holding both homes would leave almost everything in Bay Area real estate.

When the answer changes. Keep and rent if the proceeds would only go into savings or Treasury-type holdings earning less than about 5.2%–5.4%, or if you expect Cupertino prices to keep rising near 5.8% a year (break-even then rises to 6.6%). Moving in is the weakest choice on money alone: in 2045 it leaves the family about $40,680 a year worse off than renting Property A out, so it only makes sense if living there is worth that much to the family.
