Executive summary

The decision. Property A (Cupertino 95014, about $3,100,000 today) is expected to pass through a trust between 2040 and 2050. On the numbers, the best plan is to sell it within about a year of inheriting, reinvest the proceeds in a diversified mix, and keep Property B (San Francisco 94122) as the family home and rental.

Why. Three rules drive the result. First, an inherited house takes a new tax basis equal to its value at death, so selling promptly produces little or no capital gain. Second, California reassesses an inherited home: the tax bill of about $10,800 a year in 2045 becomes $81,530 if the house is rented and $62,970 even if the Heir moves in and claims the family-home exclusion. Third, at benchmark rents the house would net only about $25,040 a year after that tax, insurance and upkeep, which is 0.36% of a $6,888,060 asset.

The test. Selling beats renting whenever the reinvested money earns more than 5.2%–5.4% a year (base home-price growth of 4.29%). Selling came out ahead in 21 of 27 combinations of inheritance year, home-price growth and investment return. It lost only where the money earned the 4.65% government-securities return while Cupertino prices grew at the base or high rate, so money that would sit in savings is better left in the house, rented out.

Property B. Keep it. It nets about $67,830 a year today and houses the family in the third unit. If it is inherited as well it will be reassessed, lifting its tax from about $3,720 to $37,530 a year in 2045; its income yield on value stays above Property A's (see the comparison table on the Decision page).

Estate tax. California and its local governments charge none. The federal exclusion is $15,000,000 per person in 2026 and $15,480,000 in 2027. Both homes fit under one exclusion in every scenario; in 2045 the base case leaves room for about $13,390,660 of cash and investments before the 40% tax starts. The estate's cash total is not in the brief, so compare it with that room.

What is not settled. 39 items are open, including the Heir's income tax position, real insurance quotes, repair costs, selling costs and several figures confirmed only from excerpts. They are listed on Flagged for review. The sensitivity table on the Decision page shows how far maintenance, selling costs and market rent each move the break-even.
