What actually decides it.

A decision-boundary view: which inputs flip the ranking, which ones a report can stop arguing about, and what would have to be true for a holding strategy to win.

Private facts withheldOfficial sources linkedFictional examples only
A decision brief, not a recommendation. Everything below is arithmetic on invented values, produced by the same model as the comparison page. Its purpose is to show which unknown facts would change the answer, so the reader knows where to spend effort. It is not advice about any real household, and it does not rank real properties.

Agree the rules in writing before the figures arrive

Why this section comes before the grids. A criterion chosen after seeing one projection is reverse-engineered from that projection. The household should agree, in writing and in advance, what would make it choose each path — then compare the figures against rules it already accepted. This page is the template; the signed record stays offline. Write the decision and the date beside each item, so a future reader knows what was chosen and why.
PathRule to agree in writing firstDecisionDateOfficial reference

Rent it outAgree the rent floor in advance: rent only if written local comparables clear the break-even rent the model publishes, and only if the household accepts the landlord duties and local rules.— not decided—HUD · small-area rent data ↗

Documented investing policy for the sale proceeds

The sale proceeds should not sit in one account or be moved all at once into stocks. A written policy, agreed before the money arrives, keeps the decision from being reverse-engineered from a single projection:

Policy elementWhat it saysWhy it mattersOfficial reference
Parking the proceedsHold the net sale proceeds in short U.S. Treasury securities (bills or notes) while the trust, basis and Proposition 19 facts are confirmed. Do not leave them in an uninsured or non-interest-bearing account.Short Treasury maturities return principal at known dates; maturing cash must be reinvested at then-available yields (reinvestment risk).TreasuryDirect · Treasury bills ↗ · TreasuryDirect · Treasury notes ↗ · TreasuryDirect · pricing ↗
Phasing into stocksMove into a diversified portfolio gradually rather than at one price. A phased entry softens a 1929- or 2008-style crash at the entry point; the cost is lower growth in a sustained rally.Phasing changes the price paid for the proceeds; it is a choice about which risk to carry, not a forecast.Federal Reserve History · Stock Market Crash of 1929 ↗ · Federal Reserve History · The Great Recession ↗
DiversificationDo not concentrate the proceeds back into a single home or a single holding. Spread across asset classes and keep the rentals and any inherited home as only part of the total portfolio.Diversification can reduce certain risks but neither guarantees returns nor prevents loss.SEC · diversification ↗
Liquidity firstKeep enough in insured deposits or short Treasury bills to cover taxes, insurance, maintenance and any estate-liquidity need before committing the rest to markets.Insured deposits are protected up to the statutory limit per institution per ownership category.FDIC · insured deposits ↗
Review annuallyRe-run the model every year with updated inputs, costs and law changes. A policy set once is not a plan kept.Changing growth, inflation, tax and rent figures can move the ranking.Congressional Budget Office ↗

This is a policy template, not advice. Each element should be confirmed with an adviser and written down with a date before the proceeds arrive.

The answer is decided by four inputs, not forty

InputRange tested on this siteWhere the answer sitsWhat it means for effortOfficial framework
Whether the trust permits a saleNot a number. A legal fact.If the trustee cannot or must not sell, every other input is irrelevant.Resolve first. See the discovery register, D01 and D02.California Probate Code §16061.7 ↗
Whether basis steps upNot a number. A legal and factual test.An immediate sale is low-tax with a step-up and heavily taxed without one.Resolve second. See D03.IRS · Rev. Rul. 2023-2 ↗
Whether family-home relief appliesOccupancy, timing and filing conditions.Without relief the assessed base resets to market value; on the invented 2045 example that raises the first-year property tax by about half.Resolve third. See D04.California BOE · fact sheet ↗
Portfolio return versus home growthReturn: 0% to 10%. Growth: 0% to 7%.Selling leads once the net portfolio return clears roughly 3%, at every growth rate tested.This is the input most reports argue about, and on this model it matters least.Freddie Mac · mortgage rates ↗

The last row is the finding worth reading twice: the portfolio rate that makes selling lead is low and stable across home-price growth, because the holding strategies are carrying a large reassessed tax bill plus upkeep. Arguing about 3% versus 5% home appreciation changes far less than establishing whether a step-up or relief applies.

Decision boundary: which strategy leads

The grid below runs every combination of home-price growth and net portfolio return the comparison page exposes, for a 24-year hold after a 2045 transfer. Each cell shows the leading strategy for that pair of assumptions. The point is the shape, not any single cell.

S = sell and reinvest, R = rent then sell, L = live then sell. Every cell is a separate run of the model. No estate tax, financing, depreciation, recapture or value of occupying the home is modeled, so the boundary is a teaching illustration rather than a boundary for a real household.
