First: a sale in the year of inheritance

Before any other comparison, the brief asks for the sale that closes in the same tax year as the inheritance. It is modeled first because it fixes the two facts every later figure depends on: the basis and the gain. An inherited home takes a basis of its fair market value at the date of death (the IRS general rule), so a sale in the same year has by construction little or no gain to tax. Three things this line-up does not settle. (1) Whether a transfer by reason of death is exempt from the county documentary transfer tax is not answered on the county page — confirm before closing (see the Taxes page). (2) A sale whose proceeds are reinvested in the stock market cannot use a like-kind exchange; §1031 reaches only real property exchanged for real property (see the Questions register). (3) If the sale slips into a later tax year, the taxable gain runs from the date-of-death value to the sale price, and the entry-risk question on the Black swans page applies.

The hold strategies include a hypothetical sale at the endpoint and assumed gain tax. Sale proceeds earn a constant after-tax return proxy; there is no additional portfolio liquidation tax. Retained-property cash flows are accumulated using the same proxy. Sell’s first-year figure is a portfolio-value change proxy, not spendable distributions. Living costs are treated as funded externally and compounded. The living paths are credited the rent an owner does not pay while occupying the home — an explicit, adjustable assumption, not a market rent. On the default synthetic inputs that credit is worth about $5,019,630 at 25 years, roughly 43% of the live path’s total. The What-if controls run the same model with the credit set to zero. On these inputs the credit decides which path leads at 10 years: remove it and a selling path leads instead. That makes it a first-order assumption, not a rounding detail, and it is why the owner’s own figure for occupying the home has to be agreed in writing before the comparison is used. No estate-tax allocation is included. These assumptions and omissions can change the ordering.
