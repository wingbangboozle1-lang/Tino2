1Are there co-beneficiaries, equalization duties, debts or liens?Rule known: the trust instrument, the lien records and the probate rules decide who may take property free of claims and when. 1What are the actual assessed values and parcel-specific tax rates?Rule known: Proposition 13 caps annual assessed-value growth at 2% and limits the property tax rate to 1% plus voter-approved debt; a change in ownership generally sets base year value to market value, subject to exclusions. Where market value falls below the factored base year value, decline-in-value relief applies.— unverifiedRead county bill and tax-rate-area details for each property.A simple percentage misses direct charges and reassessment timing.BOE · Prop 13 explanation ↗

1How much are lawful market rent, vacancy and management costs for A?Rule known: HUD small-area fair market rents are ZIP-code benchmarks calculated for Housing Choice Voucher payment standards. They are not a rent quote and do not determine a lawful rent.— unverifiedGet independent current comparables and professional management estimates.Rental economics and legal limitations can change the ranking.HUD · small-area rent data ↗
1What are the current leases and rent-control status for B?Rule known: for covered San Francisco units the general annual allowable increase is 1.6% from 1 March 2026 through 28 February 2027. 2Who pays utilities and what are actual bills?Rule known: Cupertino supplies water through three separate systems — California Water Services, San Jose Water Company and the city-operated system — so a ZIP code does not identify the provider or the rate schedule.— unverifiedReview a year of bills, meter counts, provider and lease terms.Water, sewer and electricity are not fixed by the ZIP code.City of Cupertino · water ↗
2What solar tariff and interconnection date apply?Rule known: PG&E’s Solar Billing Plan produces a monthly statement and an annual True-Up statement. The tariff a system keeps and the value of any export credit depend on when the system was authorised to operate, so a monthly credit is not assured.— unverifiedReview interconnection agreement and annual true-up privately.Future export credits and fixed charges may differ.PG&E · solar billing ↗
2What repairs and capital improvements are likely?Rule known: rental expenses are deductible only if the property is rented or held for production of income; capital work is generally depreciated rather than deducted, and whether an item is a repair or an improvement is a question of fact that the records decide.— unverifiedObtain inspections, multi-year invoices and contractor estimates.Maintenance, capital basis and one-off risks affect both costs and tax.IRS · residential rental property ↗
2What is land versus building value if A is rented?Rule known: land is not depreciable; the building portion is. The allocation between them is an appraisal and tax-preparer question, not a published figure.— unverifiedUse a defensible appraisal and tax preparer’s depreciation schedule.Depreciation and later recapture can materially change the comparison.IRS · residential rental property ↗
2What are the income, brackets and potential passive-loss limits?Rule known: the comparison page uses a flat scenario rate. The actual tax follows the taxpayer’s own brackets, other income and the passive-loss rules in Publication 527.— unverifiedPrepare confidential taxpayer-specific scenarios with a CPA.Flat scenario rates are not the actual combined tax.IRS · residential rental property ↗
2What is the type and beneficiary treatment of inherited cash or retirement accounts?Rule known: beneficiaries of an IRA and most plans may take a lump-sum distribution at any time and must include taxable distributions in gross income; a designated beneficiary who is not an eligible designated beneficiary follows the 10-year rule. Cash and securities carry no equivalent beneficiary rule.— unverifiedInventory account types and designations privately with an adviser.IRA distributions and cash or securities have different tax treatment.IRS · retirement beneficiaries ↗
2What is the desired living arrangement and the value of housing services?Rule known: none — no official source governs this question. It is a household preference, and the financial ranking on the comparison page deliberately omits the value of housing services.— preference not establishedCompare nonfinancial needs, work, accessibility and alternate housing privately.A financial ranking omits the benefit of living in a home.SEC · diversification ↗
2How would sale proceeds be invested, and how much liquidity is needed?Rule known: FDIC insurance covers depositor accounts dollar-for-dollar up to the limit; for a trust owner with five or more beneficiaries that limit is $1,250,000 per owner for all trust accounts held at the same bank. Diversification and asset allocation can reduce risk but do not guarantee returns.— preference not establishedSet an investment policy, risk tolerance and deposit-insurance plan.Returns, tax drag and sequence risk are assumptions, not guarantees.FDIC · insured deposits ↗
3What laws, valuation and insurance conditions will apply at the event?Rule known: the official baseline is CBO’s February 2026 outlook for 2026–2036, in which PCE inflation returns to about 2% by 2030. It is a ten-year budget outlook, not a 50-year housing or portfolio forecast, and future law is unknown.— not knowable yetRecheck official sources regularly and immediately before a decision.Future law and prices can make today’s model obsolete.Congressional Budget Office ↗

The four facts that decide the ranking

Most of the register above improves accuracy. Four items can change which strategy wins, and they should be resolved in this order.

Order of resolution

Step 1

Does the trust permit a sale?

D01 and D02. If the trustee cannot or must not sell, the rest of the comparison is academic.

Step 2

Is there a basis step-up?

D03. An unadjusted basis turns an immediate sale from a low-tax event into a large one, which can lose the sale its first place.

Step 3

Is family-home relief available?

D04. Without occupancy, the assessed base resets to market value; on the invented example that raises the first-year property tax by about half.

Step 4

Is the estate over the filing threshold?

D05. If it is, estate tax and liquidity become constraints that no strategy comparison on this site models.
