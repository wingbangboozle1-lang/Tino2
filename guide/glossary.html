Terms used in this guide

TermPlain meaningWhy it matters hereOfficial source
Basis (income-tax)The starting figure for gain or loss on a later sale. For inherited property the IRS FAQ gives the general rule as fair market value on the date of death, with an alternate-valuation exception.Decides the tax on any later sale of the inherited home. Assessed value is a different number.IRS · inherited basis ↗
Change in ownership (property tax)A transfer that can reset assessed value to current market value unless an exclusion applies.Determines whether the property-tax bill resets after the transfer.California BOE · Prop 19 ↗
Change-in-ownership statementThe signed form filed with the county recorder or assessor for a transfer of real property. For a transfer by reason of death through a trust, it is filed within 150 days after the date of death.Sets a hard filing date that the trustee or transferee must meet.CA Revenue & Taxation Code §480 ↗
Date of deathThe date the owner died.Starts the deadlines on the Timeline page.IRS · estate tax ↗
Dollar-cost averagingInvesting equal amounts at regular intervals regardless of market ups and downs; the investor buys more shares when prices are low and fewer when they are high.The phased path in the entry-risk test is a form of dollar-cost averaging.SEC Investor.gov · dollar-cost averaging ↗
Family-home transfer (Proposition 19)A transfer of a principal residence from parent to child, where the child makes it a principal residence. The BOE page sets a value limit built from the factored base-year value plus an allowance. The allowance is adjusted over time; the current amount is not stated here (see Flagged for Review).The main route to limited reassessment for an inherited home. The guide does not conclude that it applies.California BOE · Prop 19 ↗
Federal filing thresholdThe gross-estate amount above which a federal estate tax return is required. The IRS page lists $15,000,000 for a 2026 death.Determines whether a federal return is needed at all.IRS · estate tax ↗ · IRS · Instructions for Form 706 ↗
Form 706The federal estate and generation-skipping transfer tax return. The executor uses it to figure the estate tax.The return the CPA and attorney must decide whether to file.IRS · About Form 706 ↗
Form 1041The income-tax return for a decedent’s estate or trust. It reports income, deductions, gains and distributions to beneficiaries.Governs trust and estate income once the property is held outside the decedent.IRS · About Form 1041 ↗
Form 4768The application for an automatic 6-month extension of time to file Form 706.Used to request the automatic 6-month extension of time to file. It is the form the IRS names for that request.IRS · Instructions for Form 706 ↗
Like-kind exchange (§1031)An exchange of real property held for productive use or investment for like-kind real property, on which federal gain is not recognized. The replacement must be identified within 45 days and the exchange completed within 180 days (or the return due date). Stocks and funds are not like-kind real property.Decides whether a sale can defer gain instead of paying it. Selling to reinvest in stocks cannot use it.U.S. Code · 26 U.S.C. §1031 (Cornell LII copy) ↗
Peak-to-trough declineThe fall from a market’s highest level to its lowest level before the next recovery. Example: the 1929 decline the Federal Reserve reports as 89 percent.The measure used to build the 1929 and 2008 crash shapes.Federal Reserve History · Stock Market Crash of 1929 ↗
Probate Code §16061.7 noticeA trustee notice to beneficiaries and heirs, due not later than 60 days following the triggering event, subject to the section’s exceptions.Sets the notice clock that runs in parallel with the estate and tax filings.California Probate Code §16061.7 ↗
ReassessmentSetting a new taxable value for property. The BOE guidance states that a family home that stops being the owner’s principal residence receives a new taxable value.The step that can move the property-tax line sharply after the transfer.California BOE · Prop 19 ↗
Tax rate area (TRA)A specific geographic area all of whose territory lies within the same combination of local agencies for the fiscal year. Santa Clara County has over 3,200 TRAs, each with a unique number and its own total rate.The parcel’s TRA decides which voter-approved debt rates are added to the 1% base rate on the bill.Santa Clara County Assessor · glossary ↗
Residential rental propertyIRS Publication 527 is titled Residential Rental Property; the guide does not restate its rules.Relevant to Property B, which the planning premise treats as rented.IRS · residential rental property ↗
Depreciation (MACRS)A yearly deduction that recovers the cost of a building used to produce income. For residential rental property the IRS requires the straight line method over 27.5 years, and only the building is depreciated: land generally does not wear out or get used up, so it cannot be depreciated.Turns part of a rental loss into a deduction now, and raises the taxable gain on a later sale.IRS · Publication 527, Residential Rental Property (2025) ↗
Mid-month conventionThe rule that fixes the first and last year of depreciation: property placed in service or disposed of during any month is treated as placed in service, or disposed of, at the midpoint of that month.A property rented from January and sold in December of a later year takes 11.5 months of a full year in each of those two years, not a whole year.IRS · Publication 527, Residential Rental Property (2025) ↗
Placed in serviceThe moment depreciation starts. Property is placed in service in a rental activity when it is ready and available for its specific use, even if it is not yet rented.The date conversions happen matters: living in a property first and then renting it starts depreciation at the change of use.IRS · Publication 527, Residential Rental Property (2025) ↗
Adjusted basisThe starting basis reduced by depreciation allowed or allowable, whether or not it was claimed, and increased by capital improvements.It is the figure subtracted from the sale proceeds, so depreciation taken over the years is repaid through a larger gain.IRS · Publication 527, Residential Rental Property (2025) ↗
Unrecaptured section 1250 gainThe part of the gain on a sale of depreciated real property that comes from the depreciation taken. The IRS states that this portion is taxed at a maximum 25% rate, above the 0%, 15% or 20% rates that apply to most capital gain.It is why a rental is not simply taxed at long-term capital-gain rates: the depreciation comes back at its own rate.IRS · Topic 409 capital gains ↗ · IRS · Instructions for Schedule D (Form 1040) (2025) ↗
Nonqualified useA period after 2008 in which neither the owner nor the owner’s spouse used the property as a main home. Gain is allocated to that period and cannot be excluded, but the period after the last date the home was a main home inside the five-year window before the sale is not counted.The order matters as much as the length: renting after living there is treated differently from renting before moving in.IRS · Publication 523, Selling Your Home (2025) ↗
Home-sale exclusion (§121)Up to $250,000 of gain on the sale of a main home is excluded from income, or $500,000 for a married couple filing jointly, where the owner owned and used the home as a residence for at least 24 months of the previous five years and has not used the exclusion in the last two years. Depreciation for periods after 6 May 1997 cannot be excluded.It is the largest single tax benefit available on the living paths, and it is lost entirely by an owner who does not live in the home inside the five-year window.IRS · Publication 523, Selling Your Home (2025) ↗
Imputed rentThe rent a household does not have to pay because it lives in a home it owns. It is real value and never appears in a cash-flow table.Crediting it is what makes a living path comparable with a renting path; the Depreciation & recapture page lets a reader switch it on and shows the break-even.IRS · Publication 527, Residential Rental Property (2025) ↗
Treasury par yield (Constant Maturity)The yield the U.S. Treasury publishes for a given maturity, derived from the daily par yield curve. Used here for the 3-month and 2-year rates.The observed parking yields on 13 November 2026 come from this series.U.S. Treasury · daily par yield curve, November 2026 ↗
Reinvestment riskThe risk that money which becomes available later has to be put to work at a lower rate than it was earning. The SEC states it for an investor whose bond is called early: they are "often faced with reinvesting the money at a lower, less attractive rate".A ladder of three-month bills is exposed to this in exactly the way a two-year note held to maturity is not. The two middle columns of the parking table on the Black swans page are the risk, priced.SEC Investor.gov · callable or redeemable bonds ↗
Treasury ladder (bills plus a note)Holding part of the cash in short bills that reprice every quarter and part in a longer note held to maturity, so some money is always coming back and part of the rate is always locked. TreasuryDirect sells bills for four to 52 weeks and notes for 2, 3, 5, 7 or 10 years.The investing policy on What decides it and the parking table on the Black swans page both use a 3-month/2-year split.TreasuryDirect · Treasury bills ↗ · TreasuryDirect · Treasury notes ↗
Months under waterThe count of months in which a portfolio is worth less than the amount put into it. A terminal value reports where you ended; this reports how long you had to wait to get there.The entry-risk table on the Black swans page uses it, because the 1929 record is a 303-month wait to regain the high rather than a shallow dip.Federal Reserve History · Stock Market Crash of 1929 ↗
Return-to-peak recovery shapeA stress shape in which the index is returned to its pre-crash level at the month the record says that happened, and only then grows at an assumed rate — instead of growing away from the low at that rate all along.It is the second version of the entry-risk test, and it is what turns 1929 from a decline into a duration.Federal Reserve History · Stock Market Crash of 1929 ↗ · Federal Reserve History · The Great Recession ↗

Each term links to an official page. Definitions are plain-language summaries of the page named in the source column, not legal advice.
