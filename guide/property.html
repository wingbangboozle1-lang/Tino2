Proposition H parcel tax — official schedule for B

San Francisco voters approved Proposition H on 3 November 2026. SFMTA publishes the FY 2027–28 annual amounts by parcel type and taxable building area, bills the first tax on fiscal year 2027–28 bills mailed in autumn 2027, and indexes the amounts with inflation from FY 2028–29. The measure runs from 1 July 2027 to 30 June 2042. The rows below are the published amounts; they are official rates, not estimates.

Why B’s amount is blank: the tax depends on the parcel type the Assessor-Recorder uses and on taxable building area. Neither can be read from a public page, so no figure is computed here. What the model does now, from 15 November 2026, is apply the official terms to B: the multifamily floor of $249 from calendar 2028, inflation-indexed from 2029 at an assumed 2% a year, and nothing before 2028 or after 2042. That is a floor, not B’s bill, and it is charged in B’s own operating line rather than omitted.

01b / the rate

Property A · tax rate area, and how to read the published rate

A California parcel’s bill is the assessed value multiplied by that parcel’s tax rate. The rate is not countywide: the county is divided into tax rate areas, and the rate is the 1% Proposition 13 maximum levy plus the voter-approved bonded indebtedness of the taxing agencies that cover that particular parcel. The county Controller-Treasurer publishes the rate for every area in its annual Property Tax Rate Book, which lists tax rates by tax rate area number.

StepWhat the official source saysWhere it comes from
What the rate is made ofThe FY 2025-26 rate book states that by law the tax rate is limited to 1% of assessed value plus amounts required to pay principal and interest on voter-approved bond indebtedness. The published summary shows a 1% Maximum Tax Levy of 1.00000, a County Retirement Levy of 0.03880 and a County Library levy of 0.00240, with school, city and district bond rates on top.Santa Clara County · FY 2025-26 tax rate book (PDF) ↗
How many areas there areThe FY 2025-26 book states there are 816 tax rate areas in the county, and that the first two digits of each tax rate area identify the city or unincorporated area in which the property is located.Santa Clara County · FY 2025-26 tax rate book (PDF) ↗
What published totals look likeTotals read from the FY 2025-26 book for individual areas in other cities run from about 1.14200% to 1.21470% per $100 of assessed value. Those are that area’s rate, not a county average and not the rate for A.Santa Clara County · FY 2025-26 tax rate book (PDF) ↗
How to read A’s own rateThe rate follows the parcel. The parcel number is on the secured tax bill and on the Assessor’s records; the rate book is then read at that tax rate area number. A ZIP code does not identify a tax rate area.Santa Clara County Controller-Treasurer · Property Tax Rate Book ↗ · Santa Clara County · Property Tax (DTAC) ↗
What this site can and cannot sayThe rate for A is left blank. No parcel number is published here, and the rate cannot be read from a public page without one; filling it in from a city or county average would be an inference.Santa Clara County Controller-Treasurer · Property Tax Rate Book ↗
What the model uses insteadThe projections use the household’s own implied effective rate — the 2025 bill divided by the 2025 assessed value — which is an observation of this parcel’s bills, not a published rate. It understates or overstates a future bill if the area’s bond levies change.BOE · Prop 13 explanation ↗

Read on 15 November 2026. The rate book page links an archive back to FY 2004-05, so the household can compare its own area across years rather than relying on one year’s figure. This is a procedure, not a rate: the number itself stays blank on purpose.
