Executive summary · Inheritance decision guide · official sources re-opened 15 Nov 2026

Read this first

Across every assumption set this site tests, selling the inherited residence and reinvesting the net proceeds finishes ahead of renting or living in it once the portfolio earns roughly three percent a year net — because the holding strategies carry a reassessed tax bill and upkeep against a rent that does not cover them. Renting is the weakest of the three on the invented numbers; living in the house is the closest rival, and the model gives no credit for the value of living there, which is the largest single omission in the arithmetic.

No path should be chosen before four facts are established, each of which can reverse that ranking. They are listed below and each is a document question, not a modelling question. The Questions register states the rule already settled for all twenty of them and leaves the household answer blank on purpose.

On the entry risk: parking sale proceeds in three-month to two-year Treasuries for 24 to 36 months and then phasing into stocks softens a crash that begins the day after the sale, and costs money in a market that keeps rising. The Black swans page shows both sides on the same table; it is a choice about which risk to carry, not a forecast.

What would change this summary

  • Any of the four facts above coming out the other way — each one is capable of reversing the ranking on its own.
  • A reassessment, an appeal, new construction or a supplemental bill, none of which the model times to the lien date. See the Properties page.
  • A parking yield materially below the observed short-Treasury rates, which the reinvestment-risk table on the Black swans page tests.
  • An enacted change to federal or California transfer rules; the watchlist on the Taxes page records what was re-read, and when.
  • A family preference about living in the house, which has real value that no table here measures.
