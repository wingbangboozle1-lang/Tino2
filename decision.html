Working conclusion (modeled — revisit at inheritance)

  • 1. Keep the San Francisco house as a rental. Low Proposition 13 tax plus ≈ $85,800/yr rental income (modeled net ≈ $77,100/yr before maintenance). The owner can live there as primary residence without losing rental income.
  • 2. Sell the Cupertino house at inheritance unless an heir needs a primary residence there. Keeping it (live-in or rental) triggers a Proposition 19 reassessment with modeled annual tax of ≈ $57,090–$70,670 — a very large carrying cost, and renting it does not clearly cover that tax (break-even ≈ $5,890/mo before other costs). Selling at stepped-up basis minimizes capital gains; proceeds (≈ $5,136,900 modeled after selling costs) can be reinvested in stocks, savings, etc.
  • 3. If an heir will live in the Cupertino home: move in within 1 year of transfer, file BOE-19-P within 3 years, and claim the homeowner's exemption within 1 year to get the partial exclusion.
  • 4. Federal estate tax is likely $0 if the total estate (houses + [INHERITANCE_ASSETS]) stays at or below $15,000,000 per person ($30,000,000 married couple with portability, 2026; indexed after). Amounts above are taxed at 40%.
Unverified inputs driving this conclusion: 3%/yr appreciation assumption; modeled tax rates 1.30% (Cupertino) / 1.22% (SF); future Prop 19 cap unknown; illustrative 5.5% selling costs; all insurance figures from non-official quotes; maintenance costs have no source (left blank); market rents not estimated; total inheritance amount unknown. Potential law changes to monitor

  • Federal estate/gift exclusion — OBBBA made $15,000,000 permanent in 2026 (indexed after 2026); future legislation could change it.
  • California Proposition 19 — could be amended by the legislature or ballot measure.
  • Capital gains step-up basis — subject to periodic federal proposals; not changed as of this research.
  • Local property tax bond rates — reset annually; verify with the county tax collector each year.
